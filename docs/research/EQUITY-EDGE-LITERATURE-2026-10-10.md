# Equity edge: primary literature and preregistration candidates

Research date: 2026-10-10. Scope: an evidence review for a separate equity study; no change to forecast-lab's weather/NBA production scope or live-trading gates. Status: literature synthesis and proposed tests, not an executed stock-level backtest or a claim of newly discovered alpha. Method: primary papers, author/institution pages, and official data documentation; Google Scholar search was attempted but returned an access error. Access limits: inspected accessible abstracts, author summaries, methodological documentation, and available paper extracts; did not read every full paper or any complete investment book.

## What the target means

A 10–20% long-run compound annual growth rate (CAGR) and a minimum 10–20% **in every year** are different claims. CAGR is `(ending wealth / starting wealth)^(1 / years) - 1`; arithmetic average returns do not capture compounding losses. For example, +50% followed by −50% averages 0%, but leaves 75% of starting wealth and gives a −13.4% two-year CAGR. None of the reviewed sources establishes the requested annual floor; momentum's documented crashes are a direct counterexample to interpreting a positive premium as a floor. [S3] Report every calendar year, worst rolling 12 months, drawdown, recovery time, and the number of years below 0%, 10%, and 20% alongside CAGR. A return spread from a long–short academic factor is not a fully funded long-only account return; collateral, leverage, short borrowing, fees, and tax treatment differ. Twenty historical years are useful evidence, but are only twenty annual observations and cannot establish a future guaranteed floor.

## Evidence map

| Family | Primary evidence | What transfers to a retail test | Important limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cross-sectional momentum | Jegadeesh–Titman document winner/loser return persistence over 3–12-month holding periods. [S1] | Rank liquid stocks on lagged returns and hold the winners. | Their long–short result is not the CAGR of buying winners; momentum can crash. [S3] |
| Intermediate momentum | Novy-Marx studies the role of returns 12–7 months before formation. [S2] | A prespecified robustness comparison to conventional 12–2 momentum. | Not permission to choose the best lookback after seeing the test period. |
| Profitability and value | Novy-Marx finds gross profits/assets predicts average returns and improves value strategies, including among large liquid firms. [S4] | Combine a simple profitability rank and valuation rank. | Accounting publication timing and restatements must be handled; a gross-profit measure differs from French OP. [S16] |
| Broader quality | Asness–Frazzini–Pedersen find historical risk-adjusted QMJ returns across the US and 24 other countries. [S5] | Use as supporting evidence for quality, not dozens of extra optimized signals. | QMJ is explicitly long quality and short junk; its definition is broader than profitability. |
| Time-series trend | Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen study 58 futures/forward contracts and own-return persistence. [S6] | A simple equity/cash trend rule is a low-complexity risk-control test. | Equity-only long/cash is an adaptation, not replication of a diversified, volatility-scaled futures strategy. |
| Earnings drift | Bernard–Thomas investigate delayed adjustment after earnings. [S7] | Test only with historical announcements and genuinely preannouncement inputs. | Transaction costs reduce the implementable spread; stronger drift can occur in more costly stocks. [S8] |
| Attention/social | Barber–Odean document attention-driven buying; Chen et al. find informative stock opinions. [S9, S11] | Treat platform, author, timestamp, and event as separate variables. | Attention, information, and bullish sentiment are not interchangeable. Robinhood crowd buying can precede losses. [S10] |

## Why these are research candidates, not promised edges

Momentum is unusually easy to specify from prices, but reversals after distressed markets create severe long–short crashes. [S1, S3] The short side contributes materially to the academic construction; excluding it changes both expected performance and risk. French's conventional momentum factor uses monthly size and prior-return sorts, with prior months 2–12 and NYSE breakpoints. [S15] Novy-Marx's intermediate-horizon result is a reason to preregister one sensitivity test, not a universal claim that a particular lookback dominates everywhere. [S2]

Gross profitability measures `(revenues − cost of goods sold) / assets`; the economic motivation for combining it with value is to avoid buying low-quality firms solely because they look cheap. [S4] Fama–French's five-factor work improves its description of average returns, but this is a risk-model finding, not a market-beating allocation guarantee. [S17] Use matched-universe benchmarks to separate stock selection from market, size, sector, and value exposures. The feasible retail advantage is a hypothesis about patience, low turnover, small trade size, and consistent implementation; this review does not demonstrate a unique information advantage.

The original time-series momentum dataset is long–short, based on 58 liquid instruments, and covers January 1985–December 2009. [S6] Replacing that strategy with one equity fund and cash forfeits the original cross-asset diversification and short exposure. An equity/cash rule can whipsaw, miss sharp recoveries, and earn little while out of stocks; its relevant test is drawdown reduction relative to the return it sacrifices. Do not retrospectively label recessions or bull/bear regimes and feed those labels into a historical trading rule.

PEAD requires the actual public release time and an entry after the information was available, not the fiscal quarter end. [S7, S8] Using an already revised analyst consensus or treating after-hours earnings as known at that day's close leaks future information. A January 2026 working paper reports postannouncement trading results consistent with efficient formation after 2016; this is contrary evidence from a particular setting, not a universal verdict that PEAD is gone. [S18] Keep PEAD outside the initial research candidates until the data and contemporary net performance are independently established.

Barber–Odean find that news, unusual volume, and extreme returns attract retail purchases. [S9] Barber et al. report −4.7% average 20-day abnormal returns for the top stocks purchased each day by Robinhood users in their sample. [S10] That is an event-study result, not an executable short strategy; borrowing costs, squeeze risk, and selection matter. Chen et al. find return and earnings-surprise information in articles and comments, showing that not all social content is mere noise. [S11] Neither result validates buying whatever is trending on Reddit or X, or an LLM sentiment score transferred across platforms and eras. A credible social test needs immutable publication/retrieval times, historical author selection, ticker mapping, deleted/edited-content policy, and postpublication executable prices. No 20-year social strategy replication was established here; a current scrape of surviving posts is insufficient.

## Buffett: useful decomposition, not a shortcut

Frazzini–Kabiller–Pedersen attribute Buffett's record to cheap, safe, high-quality stocks plus persistent financing; their study estimates about 1.6-to-1 leverage and a 0.76 Sharpe ratio. [S12] Low-cost insurance float and the ability to endure large drawdowns are part of that mechanism; ordinary retail margin is not an equivalent input. The research is retrospective explanation of an exceptional survivor, not evidence that a simple unlevered screen should earn Buffett's historical CAGR. It supports studying quality at reasonable prices and discipline, not a 20% yearly minimum or copying delayed holdings disclosures as if they were real-time trades.

## Three rules to freeze before stock-level testing

These are deliberately simple author proposals motivated by the literature, not exact reproductions or statistically validated recommendations. Shared equity universe: US exchange-listed ordinary common shares, top 1,000 by prior-month market capitalization, prior close at least $5, prior 60-session median daily dollar volume at least $5m. Determine all membership and eligibility historically; include subsequently delisted firms, historical share classes, cash merger proceeds, dividends, splits, and delisting outcomes. No shorting or leverage. Use total-return observations for signals and realistic unadjusted tradable prices plus corporate actions for execution/accounting. Freeze these thresholds before running results; they are design choices, not literature-estimated optimal values.

### A. Buffered long-only momentum

1. At each month-end, rank eligible stocks by compounded total return for the 11 complete months ending one month before formation (12–2 momentum).
2. Initialize with the top 50; subsequently retain eligible holdings ranked in the top 100 and fill vacancies from the highest-ranked nonholdings to 50.
3. Equal-weight at each monthly rebalance; resolve tied scores by a stable security identifier, not the current ticker.
4. Signal after the final close; trade at the next session's open with stated spread/slippage costs, including overnight price movement.
5. Required point-in-time (PIT) data: historical universe, security IDs, market caps, daily prices/volume, corporate actions, delisting returns.
6. Compare with equal-weight and value-weight versions of the same eligible universe, as well as the broad market; report turnover and sector exposures.
7. One robustness variant only: replace the lookback with months 12–7; do not select between rules using the reported evaluation period. [S1–S3, S13]

### B. Annual value plus profitability

1. At June-end, apply the shared universe, exclude financial firms by the contemporaneous industry code, and require positive book equity and positive assets.
2. Use the latest original annual filing publicly available before that June-end close, with fiscal year-end in the preceding calendar year; exclude missing inputs.
3. Compute equal-weight percentile ranks of book equity/current market equity and annual gross profit/total assets; average the two percentile ranks.
4. Buy the top 50, equal-weighted, at the next session's open; hold to the following June rebalance except corporate actions or liquidation.
5. Required PIT data: original statement values, fiscal periods, filing acceptance timestamps, historical industry codes, plus all data required by A.
6. Benchmark against the same nonfinancial eligible universe and simple value-only/profitability-only diagnostic portfolios; disclose all diagnostics rather than selecting the winner.
7. Do not silently substitute operating-profitability data for gross-profit data; a French OP series is a separately labeled proxy. [S4, S16]

### C. Broad-equity trend with Treasury bills

1. At each month-end, compound the preceding 12 complete months of a broad US equity total-return series and of Treasury bills over the same interval.
2. Hold the equity vehicle if its 12-month compounded return exceeds the bills' return; otherwise hold bills. Rebalance at the next session's open.
3. No leverage, volatility target, shorting, optimized regime model, or discretionary override.
4. Required data: tradable fund history from inception, distribution/corporate-action history, contemporaneous bill returns, expense ratios, execution timing/costs.
5. If using a research index and French RF, label the output a hypothetical index/cash proxy; monthly data cannot validate next-open execution or intra-month drawdowns.
6. Primary hypothesis: lower maximum drawdown than buy-and-hold; report its opportunity cost in CAGR and missed recoveries, without requiring superior return. [S6]

## Costs, falsification, and what a 20-year backtest can establish

Novy-Marx–Velikov find buy/hold buffers useful, transaction costs materially reduce profitability, and high-turnover anomalies are particularly vulnerable. [S13] Frazzini–Israel–Moskowitz find lower costs in a large institutional live-trade sample than some earlier estimates; costs should be measured, not universally assumed fatal or zero. [S14] Predeclare a base execution model and, for sensitivity, costs of 10/25/50 basis points per traded dollar; these are stress assumptions, not measured quotes. Charge both buys and sells; distinguish one-way turnover from sum of absolute traded weights; add fund expense drag where the input does not include it. Show pretax results, turnover, and account-specific after-tax scenarios separately; no universal tax-rate claim. Use the latest twenty complete calendar years as a fixed descriptive window, with earlier data reserved for signal warmup; mark partial years separately. Show subperiods and walk-forward diagnostics, but call them retrospective validation: a split chosen today is not a genuinely untouched future sample. Any strategy/parameter selection must occur before examining the relevant test segment; log every attempted variant and failed result. McLean–Pontiff report predictor returns 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication across 97 predictors, illustrating both selection effects and decay. [S19] Harvey–Liu–Zhu argue for materially stronger significance hurdles with many tested factors; their greater-than-3 t-statistic example is not a blanket guarantee. [S20] Reject a claimed edge if it depends on today's constituents, corrected historical fundamentals, same-close execution, omitted delistings, zero costs, or one best-selected lookback. Archive download dates and hashes: official historical data can be revised, so a research portfolio downloaded today is not a complete vintage PIT reconstruction. [S21] French portfolio series support a useful free historical screen, but do not supply holdings-level trading, our specific universe, or our actual account experience. [S15, S16, S21] SEC XBRL first became mandatory in 2009; its frames API selects the last-filed matching fact, so current frames are not an as-known-then 20-year fundamental database. [S22] For B, a full 2006–2025 stock-level study therefore needs historical filings reconstruction or verified PIT fundamentals beyond a naive current SEC API query.

## ETF controls and screened books

For the available ETF experiment, freeze SPY buy-and-hold, monthly equal-weight original nine sector ETFs, top-three sector 12–1 momentum, SPY 10-month moving-average timing, and momentum gated by that same SPY rule before viewing outcomes. These are controls/adaptations; the stock-level rules above remain untested proposals. Moskowitz–Grinblatt establish original industry-momentum evidence, but nine sector ETFs are not their industry universe or portfolio construction. Faber's original 2006 white paper/2007 journal article motivates a monthly 10-month SMA rule; his author FAQ says the length was representative, not optimized. [S23, S24] In our frozen ETF protocol, “12–1” means C[t−1]/C[t−12]−1 at month-end t: eleven months of returns, excluding the most recent month. This is the skipped-month convention; the formula, rather than the label, controls. Use only a signal known before execution. Zero-yield cash is a simplifying assumption that understates the return available from bills when yields are positive; do not call it a Treasury-bill replication. These ETF controls cannot establish individual-stock picking, PEAD, fundamentals, or social-data alpha. Better drawdowns or a higher Sharpe ratio may reflect reduced market exposure and systematic risk management; attribute return differences against equal-weight sectors and SPY rather than simply naming them alpha. Books screened, not fully read: Ilmanen's *Investing Amid Low Expected Returns* (2022), official excerpt contents covering portfolio construction, risk management, costs, and timing; Carver's *Systematic Trading*, author introduction/contents and companion resources covering fitting, allocation, trading speed, sizing, and costs. These provide process references, not independent proof of any annual return target. [S25, S26]

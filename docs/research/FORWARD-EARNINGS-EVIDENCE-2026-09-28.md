# Forward earnings evidence: hypothesis, costs and observation time

Research checked 2026-09-28 UTC. Scope: the prospective, paper-only experiment in [the fixed plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-09-28-forward-earnings-health.md). This note does not establish profitable execution or authorize trades.

## What the literature supports

Post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD) is a reason to test delayed information response. It is not evidence that our exact screen works. The papers below study different populations, signals, holding periods and execution assumptions.

| Primary source | Finding and relevance | Limit for this experiment |
|---|---|---|
| DellaVigna and Pollet, *Investor Inattention, Firm Reaction, and Friday Earnings Announcements*, NBER WP 11683 (2005): [paper record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11683), [original paper archived by the Library of Congress](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/05/62/07/67/2005620767/2005620767.pdf) | Friday announcements have weaker immediate and stronger delayed responses, consistent with limited attention. | Historical evidence about announcement timing; it does not validate today's next-open, 20-session screen or its thresholds. |
| Novy-Marx, *Fundamentally, Momentum is Fundamental Momentum*, NBER WP 20984 (2015): [author abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20984) | Earnings momentum explains substantial price-momentum performance in the studied constructions. | Motivation for an earnings-versus-momentum comparison, not proof that combining our two filters adds return. |
| Luo, Ravina, Sammon and Viceira, *Retail Investors' Contrarian Behavior Around News, Attention, and the Momentum Effect*, NBER WP 34086 (2025): [author abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34086); [earlier Chicago Fed working paper](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/working-papers/2023/2023-34) | Retail contrarian trading intensity helps explain drift; extreme surprises and return streaks alone are insufficient in the authors' analysis. | We do not observe their brokerage-account flows. A recent publication is not proof of a current implementable edge from our simpler inputs. The earlier working paper is a different version. |
| Ng, Rusticus and Verdi, *Implications of Transaction Costs for the Post-Earnings Announcement Drift*, Journal of Accounting Research 46(3), 661–696 (2008): [journal abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1475-679X.2008.00290.x) | Higher costs are associated with more drift, while implementation costs significantly reduce strategy profits. | This supports skepticism about gross returns; it does not say every strategy loses after costs. Our 10bp per side is an assumption, not a cost estimate established by this paper. |
| Christensen, Timmermann and Veliyev, *Warp Speed Price Moves: Jumps after Earnings Announcements* (2026): [version 2 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08962v2), [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08962) | In their large-US-firm after-hours experiment, 2016–2020 gains are not significantly positive after bid–ask spreads; latency further erodes performance. | The sample ends in 2020. This is not evidence that every PEAD strategy stopped working after 2016, nor a direct test of our next-open, 20-session rule. |

The NBER 20984 and 34086 findings above use their indexed primary abstracts; direct page retrieval was blocked. The Friday-announcement paper and the recent after-hours paper were inspected as full text. Journal abstract claims are not treated as a replication.

## What is fixed, and what remains unproven

The plan predeclares a positive EPS estimate, at least a 5% beat, positive 20-session excess momentum, a $5 price floor, $20 million median daily dollar-volume floor and at most three selections. These are research choices, not empirically optimized or literature-validated cutoffs. Keep them fixed before unseen outcomes and preserve the failed broad study. Report the same event-conditioned universe's momentum-only ablation alongside the earnings filter and matched SPY. That comparison isolates the added filter more closely; it does not represent the entire market.

The existing broad historical study did not establish an advantage. A future positive cohort would not reverse that conclusion by itself. Daily cohorts can overlap, so their count is not an independent sample size. Report losses, empty selections, excluded or missing names, pending horizons, benchmark returns and costs. Current-universe survivorship, revisions, execution uncertainty and incomplete calendars remain limitations. Never turn a screen score into a calibrated probability.

## Fiscal period is not information availability

SEC submissions and XBRL APIs update as filings are disseminated. Company-facts data aggregate reported facts; frames select a last-filed fact matching a requested period. Fiscal calendars and units vary. A current response is not an archived point-in-time snapshot, and a quarter's end does not say when its results became public. [SEC EDGAR API documentation](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces).

The SEC distinguishes report period, filing date, acceptance time and correction date. It does not supply an exact public-website availability timestamp; dissemination delay varies. An acceptance timestamp is therefore useful provenance but not proof that our process possessed the information then. [SEC webmaster FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/about/webmaster-frequently-asked-questions).

For this experiment, retain source URL/provider, accession and form when present, fiscal period, publication/acceptance metadata, actual UTC retrieval time, first archived observation, content hash and method/decision time. Preserve subsequent versions. Use facts only after actual observation; do not backdate them to a reported fiscal period or overwrite the evidence behind an earlier decision. For a date-only announcement, the following UTC midnight is a conservative publication floor, still subject to the later actual retrieval/observation time. A calendar entry alone does not verify that results were released.

A consensus estimate fetched after the announcement may already have changed. Without a preserved pre-announcement estimate, describe the result as a comparison against the **currently captured estimate**, not a reconstructed historical surprise. Check quarter, currency, units and basic/diluted or adjusted/GAAP comparability; unavailable provenance is a limitation or exclusion, not a value to invent. An official release confirming adjusted actual EPS does not establish that an unlabeled provider estimate uses the same accounting basis; a numerical beat can therefore reflect incompatible measures. Earnings-calendar coverage is also only the provider's captured coverage, even when the query is unfiltered.

These are design requirements inferred from the source semantics, not guarantees supplied by the SEC or provider. Decisions begin at their real creation time; evaluation begins at a subsequent eligible regular-session open. No retrospective label, official filing citation or existing price bar substitutes for that prospective boundary.

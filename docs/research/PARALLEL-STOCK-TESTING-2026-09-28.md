# Multiple-testing controls for nine parallel stock paper books

**Scope, 2026-09-28.** The approved design freezes three families (momentum held 5 or 20 sessions; earnings-conditioned momentum held 20) crossed with 10-, 20-, and 60-session lookbacks. All nine start on one future capture, use separate $100 virtual accounts and the same event-conditioned catalogue/calendar intersection. The 60-*trading-session* checkpoint is a first operational and descriptive review, **not** a minimum track record that proves alpha, a winner, or real-money readiness. [Frozen design](../superpowers/specs/2026-09-28-parallel-stock-books-design.md).

## What the primary research says

- Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu define probability of backtest overfitting as the chance that a strategy selected as best in sample falls below the median out of sample; their combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV) estimates this using repeated in/out partitions. Their method is for a sufficiently long *historical strategy-performance matrix*. Nine short, prospectively started paper ledgers do not yet supply that matrix, and applying CSCV now would create false precision. [Original paper, author-hosted PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf); [journal record](https://doi.org/10.21314/jcf.2016.322).
- Bailey and López de Prado's deflated Sharpe ratio (DSR) adjusts a selected Sharpe for multiple trials, sample length, and non-normality. It needs the performance of unselected trials and an estimate of *independent* trials; reporting only the best book or entering `N=9` without examining dependence would misuse it. We should retain all nine return streams and the prior research log, then assess whether DSR is estimable only after substantially more prospective observations. [Original paper, author-hosted PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf).
- Harvey, Liu and Zhu show why conventional single-test significance can be misleading after many finance hypotheses and explicitly accommodate correlated tests. Their numerical factor-discovery hurdle is **not** a ready-made threshold for these tiny account ledgers. The relevant implication is to account for the whole search, not to label a favorable one of nine as validated. [Original NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592.pdf).
- White's Reality Check addresses inference about the *best* model found after a specification search rather than pretending the winner was the only model tried. A future formal best-of-nine analysis would need synchronized net-return series and a dependence-aware resampling design; the current 60-session checkpoint cannot support a credible test. [Original Econometrica article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-0262.00152).
- Lo derives how serial correlation changes Sharpe-ratio inference and time aggregation. Here 5- and 20-session holdings, common symbols, shared capture days, and repeated daily decisions make both within-book returns and between-book outcomes dependent. Nine accounts are not nine independent replications; a count of trades is not a count of independent observations. [Original paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=377260).

## Reporting rule for this experiment

1. Freeze and timestamp all nine protocol IDs, lookbacks, holding periods, account launch times, source hashes, and the two intended *paired* contrasts: momentum 5 versus momentum 20 at matched lookback (holding period), and momentum 20 versus earnings 20 at matched lookback (earnings gate). Disclose that the broader historical momentum run failed and that the current challenger was designed after seeing earlier data; predeclaration of these nine variants prevents **further** tuning but does not erase that research history.
2. At every review, publish **all nine**, including empty, losing, paused, stale, invalid, and unmeasured books. Give net marked equity, realized and unrealized P&L, drawdown, cash exposure, 10-basis-point-per-side cost assumptions, passive SPY and matched-trade comparisons, distinct entry dates, completed exits, and overlapping exposure. Compare like-for-like launch dates and lookbacks; disclose that a fully invested SPY reference is not risk-matched to cash-heavy books. Do not rank only on realized profits while open losses remain.
3. At 60 completed market sessions from common launch, audit data capture, bookkeeping, rule adherence, and descriptive differences. Avoid annualized returns or Sharpe claims, unadjusted winner p-values, and a “best strategy” declaration. Twenty-session holds can have few effectively distinct outcomes in this window, while daily overlapping positions reuse market moves. This is an inference from the frozen design and the dependence cautions above, **not** a calculated effective sample size.
4. If a signal looks promising, keep the nine rules frozen for a fresh, longer prospective period; define the outcome, benchmark, dependence treatment, and multiplicity correction *before* examining that new period. Version any rule change as a new experiment with a new start. A later joint-date/block analysis must retain cross-book correlation and all tried variants; its confidence bounds and power must be checked from observed variance rather than assuming 60 sessions suffice.

**Material design concern:** the event-conditioned universe and common signals make the books highly correlated, while 10/20/60-session lookbacks may have unequal coverage. A raw best-of-nine ranking conflates rule quality, market regime, history availability, and cash exposure. The planned separate ledgers and shared immutable captures make these differences inspectable; they do not themselves establish predictive advantage.

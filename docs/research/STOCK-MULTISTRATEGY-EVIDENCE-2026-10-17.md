# Multi-strategy stock research: evidence and data boundary

Research date: October 17, 2026. This note records a bounded next research direction before new candidate results. It activates no strategy, account, feed or execution. Combining economically different ideas is sensible to test; maximizing the already-observed backtest is not a reliable objective.

## What has actually been tested

The [first twenty-year results](STOCK-20YEAR-RESULTS-2026-09-30.md) cover October 2, 2006–September 28, 2026. Its seven arms are momentum excluding the latest week (M), five-session reversal (R), low twenty-session volatility (V), and equal-percentile blends MR, MV, RV and MRV. They use the same eligible stocks. The [frozen design](../superpowers/specs/2026-09-30-long-stock-research-design.md) crosses three selection sizes and three holds: 63 rules, repeated at three costs to produce 189 rows.

At 10bp per side, 50/63 histories were complete and 17 had positive average cash-matched SPY excess. No rule had positive average excess in all four declared time blocks. All seven arms and losing/incomplete results remain evidence. These are independently reset $100 cohorts, not compounded twenty-year accounts or annual returns. The sample selected 100 current survivors; dividends, terminal delisting outcomes and original dollar eligibility were not reconstructed. The price-only blends therefore already exist, but have not demonstrated a robust advantage. None contains valuation, original filing-based quality, a market trend exposure gate, historical news surprises or options pricing.

## Distinct mechanisms worth testing

| Mechanism | Primary evidence | What it would add, and what it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-sectional momentum plus a market trend gate | Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen distinguish own-return trend from relative ranking; their study covers 58 futures/forward contracts and uses past twelve-month excess returns. [Authors' paper page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Time-Series-Momentum). | A stock can rank first while the whole market falls. An exposure gate could avoid some such periods, but may miss sharp recoveries. A long-only SPY gate is our new hypothesis, not a replication of their diversified futures strategy. |
| Momentum plus portfolio risk control | Moreira and Muir study lower exposure when factor volatility rises. [NBER abstract](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208). | Portfolio exposure scaling differs from selecting low-volatility stocks or dividing a stock score by volatility. It requires lagged risk estimates and explicit cash/leverage rules; its published results do not establish our account returns. |
| Value plus quality plus momentum | Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen document complementary value/momentum factor behavior across markets. [Authors' paper page](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/journal-article/value-and-momentum-everywhere). Asness, Frazzini and Pedersen's quality construct considers profitability, growth, safety and payout. [Author-hosted QMJ paper](https://images.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/Working-Papers/Quality-Minus-Junk.pdf). | Adds economic inputs distinct from recent price movement. Cheapness, profitability and strength need separate component controls. A three-rank long-only blend is a new test, not a promised replication of published long/short factors. Today's restated fundamentals cannot stand in for information known twenty years ago. |
| Reversal with liquidity and cost discipline | Frazzini, Israel and Moskowitz estimate trading costs using institutional execution data; short-term reversal is especially sensitive to costs, unlike their optimized value/momentum implementations. [Authors' paper page](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Trading-Costs-of-Asset-Pricing-Anomalies). | Existing R is already a reversal signal. More turnover or microcaps are not automatically more opportunity. A liquidity-gated challenger needs historical unadjusted prices, volume, spreads or explicit conservative proxies, next-open execution and no silent omission of halted/delisted names. Institutional estimates are not retail fill guarantees. |

## Candidate menu recorded now, not activated

Keep the existing seven-arm replay unchanged as the baseline. The next finite candidate set is:

1. **Trend/risk decomposition:** the existing M component; M with one fixed market trend gate; M with one fixed volatility exposure rule; and M with both. Compare all four on identical dates/universe and also against SPY with the identical exposure schedule. That second benchmark distinguishes stock selection from simply holding more cash in falling markets. Price history can support an exploratory version, subject to the current survivor/dividend limitations.
2. **Value/quality decomposition:** value, quality, momentum, and one equal-weight rank blend. Use a common available-information universe. This is blocked for a defensible twenty-year stock-level test until original filing availability, historical shares/valuation denominators and delisted-inclusive returns are verified. Missing features must remain missing.
3. **Reversal/liquidity decomposition:** existing reversal with a fixed eligibility rule versus the same rule with one predeclared stricter liquidity screen, retaining both complete and unavailable outcomes. It tests sensitivity to trading feasibility, not a threshold search until profits appear.

The following bounded specification replaces the open choices for the first two candidates. Reversal/liquidity stays a later idea, outside this test budget. Before execution, copy this specification and the exact source identities into an immutable manifest; an implementation failure cannot silently change the rules. None of these changes the current nine account rules or their risk caps.

## Predeclared follow-on specification: `combo-followon-v1`

**Purpose and interval.** Test incremental benefit over components, not find the largest return. Replay October 2, 2006–October 16, 2026, requesting warm-up from September 1, 2005. If required evidence is unavailable, report the actual shorter coverage or block that comparison; do not fabricate warm-up. The whole earlier sample was already inspected, so this is a retrospective diagnostic even with newly frozen parameters. There is no pristine historical holdout left in it.

**Shared mechanics.** Use five stocks, three fixed holding periods of 21/63/105 genuine benchmark sessions (approximately one/three/five months), and no overlapping cohorts within a horizon. Decide after a completed close, enter next regular open and exit at the horizon close; start the next decision after that exit. Each cohort starts with $100, at most $50 invested equally, idle cash earning zero, fractional shares assumed. Include proportional costs at 10/25/50bp each side; 25bp is the primary scenario. No shorts, leverage, stops, optimized weights or mid-hold substitutions. Missing required observations retain an unavailable cohort. Publish component/blend results even when negative. These are cohort returns, not account CAGR; daily account equity and sequential cash would need a separately specified account engine.

**Price-only experiment: four arms.** Preserve the original deterministic 100-symbol sample and original common stock/price/liquidity eligibility rules, including their disclosed split-adjusted historical-price proxy. Require five eligible names, 61 aligned stock closes and 200 genuine SPY closes. Rank all four arms using the identical existing momentum formula, `log(stock[t-5]/stock[t-60]) - log(SPY[t-5]/SPY[t-60])`, with symbol-order ties. No positive-score filter. Define `T = 1` when SPY's latest close is strictly above its trailing 200-close arithmetic average, otherwise zero. Define `s = sample_std(last 20 SPY daily log returns, ddof=1) * sqrt(252)` and `R = min(1, 0.10/s)`; invalid or zero `s` makes the whole common-date comparison unavailable. Allocation multipliers are **1, T, R and T*R**. Each selected stock receives $10 times its multiplier, all-in including entry friction. Gate/risk values are fixed at cohort creation; a zero multiplier stays cash for that cohort. This tests entry-time exposure discipline, not a daily crash exit. Long holds can therefore experience a downturn that begins after entry, and the gate can miss recoveries. Freeze these single windows/target without surrounding parameter searches.

**Conditional fundamental experiment: four arms.** Run only after original filing availability, security/share identity, unadjusted eligibility, corporate actions and delisted terminal outcomes pass an inclusive-data audit. Use the historically active US ordinary-common-stock universe, excluding financial issuers by contemporaneous classification; classify unknowns as unavailable. Require price at least $5, trailing 20-session median dollar volume at least $20m, and all three features. Value is latest available positive common book equity divided by contemporaneous market capitalization. Quality is latest available annual gross profit divided by positive total assets from that same annual filing. Momentum uses the exact 60-to-5-session formula above. Financial features become usable only after one full regular session following the recorded filing date and expire after 550 calendar days; amendments enter only after their own availability. Rank each feature ascending using average percentile ranks. Compare **value, quality, momentum and their equal-weight mean percentile**, taking five names with symbol-order ties, at the same 50% maximum allocation and shared horizons/costs. This quality proxy is not the full QMJ factor. One feature's missingness excludes the name from every arm; publish that exclusion rate. Do not fill gaps with zero or current restatements.

**Budget and comparisons.** Each experiment has four arms × three horizons × three costs: 36 rows, at most 72 total when data permits, not 72 independent discoveries. No additional sizes/weights/thresholds in this version. For every cohort construct SPY with exactly the same entry/exit dates, invested dollars, idle cash and transaction-cost assumptions. Also show unchanged 50%-invested SPY and fully invested SPY as descriptive references. Exposure-matched SPY separates stock choice from cash timing; the fixed-exposure references show the cost or benefit of reduced exposure. Risk matching is incomplete because stock baskets retain different betas/concentration: disclose that and report realized risk rather than calling excess return alpha.

**Evidence required before a future paper trial.** Require reproduced accounting/no-lookahead tests, audited inclusive returns and timed fundamentals where used, complete missing-outcome reporting, and positive mean matched-SPY excess at the primary 25bp scenario with the family-wise 95% lower confidence bound above zero: use a one-sided bootstrap percentile at 0.05/24 across the 24 planned arm/horizon comparisons. Use synchronized decision-calendar-year bootstrap blocks, 10,000 draws and seed 1729, retaining all cohort rows within sampled years and dependence across arms. Require at least ten complete year blocks; any unavailable mature outcome withholds full-history qualification rather than dropping the trade. Publish 10/50bp sensitivity and four fixed periods (2006–2012, 2013–2019, 2020–2025, 2026), plus component comparisons and worst cohorts. No calendar block may have negative mean excess for a candidate to qualify under this deliberately conservative screen. Passing earns only a proposal for a frozen, genuinely future paper trial with 21/63/105-session reviews; it does not trigger an order or establish profitability. Historical uncertainty remains exploratory because earlier research already influenced these hypotheses. No candidate passing is an acceptable result. A 66% annual target, searching until winners appear, or repeatedly changing the pass conditions would invalidate this protocol.

The [existing data audit](STOCK-20YEAR-DATA-2026-09-30.md) measured Robinhood requests: two ten-year chunks worked; one twenty-year request exceeded the 5,000 estimated-bar cap; LEH/BSC were not found; synthetic prelisting rows existed. That is useful exploratory price access, not inclusive market coverage. This review did not purchase or authenticate a new data feed.

## Validation target

Measure net performance, matched exposure/cash SPY excess, turnover, concentration, unavailable outcomes and stability across downturn/recovery intervals. A lower market exposure can reduce losses without demonstrating stock-selection skill. Keep crashes and rebounds, and avoid defining regimes using information available only afterward for trading decisions; retrospective regime labels are diagnostic only.

Preserve all earlier tests in the trial log. Bailey and coauthors explain selection-induced backtest overfitting; Harvey, Liu and Zhu show why ordinary single-test thresholds are inadequate in a large factor search. [PBO manuscript](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf), [multiple-testing paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592). Freeze chronological development/evaluation boundaries, purge labels that cross them, and use date-block uncertainty estimates when data permit. The inspected 2006–2026 replay is no longer untouched evidence. A candidate needs genuinely subsequent observations before promotion; a lucky historical maximum is not the stopping condition.
